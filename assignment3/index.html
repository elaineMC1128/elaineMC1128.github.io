Symptom: Misplacement

People with Alzheimer's often place objects in unusual locations — a remote in the fridge, keys in a cereal box. They cannot retrace their steps to find them, and may believe someone else has taken them.

Symptom: Spatial Disorientation

Alzheimer's gradually damages the brain's spatial reasoning. Patients may become completely lost in a neighbourhood they've lived in for decades — unsure where they are, how they got there, or how to get home.

Symptom: Repetitive Behaviour

Due to severe short-term memory loss, patients repeat the same questions or stories within minutes. They are not doing it deliberately — their brain simply cannot store the fact that the question was already asked and answered.

Symptom: Memory Loss

Alzheimer's erases memory in reverse chronological order. Recent events disappear first — what they had for breakfast, a conversation from an hour ago. Distant memories can remain vivid for years. Eventually, even those fade.
